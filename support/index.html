Frequently Asked Questions

Will my customers know they’re talking to a robot?

Yes. Bolt always tells your caller it’s a smart assistant, right up front — never pretends to be human. Some operators worry that upfront disclosure will scare callers off. In practice, most callers care more about getting a fast, helpful answer than about who’s on the other end. Bolt is built to be useful in the first ten seconds, not to trick anyone.

Can it handle a real emergency, or just routine calls?

Bolt’s smart assistant is trained to recognize the calls that can’t wait — no heat or no A/C, carbon monoxide fumes, flooding or water damage, a burst pipe, a sparking outlet, and similar serious situations. When a call like that comes in, Bolt gets the details and escalates it to you right away — push notification, then a text, then an automated call if you still haven’t seen it. What Bolt doesn’t do is make the judgment calls that need your expertise on site. It’s built to flag and get you moving fast, not to diagnose or promise a timeline your customer can hold you to.

Do I have to give up my phone number?

No. You keep your number — whatever number you already give out to customers. Most operators run their business off their cell phone, so Bolt is built around that: you forward your existing number to your smart assistant, same way you’d forward calls to voicemail. No separate business line required. You can turn forwarding off anytime by dialing a code.

How much does it cost, and is there a contract?

Your first month is free. The next two months are discounted. After that, you’re on standard pricing. No long-term contract. See current pricing for exact numbers.

How long does setup take? Do I need a computer?

About 10 minutes, start to finish, right from your phone. No laptop, no sitting down at a desk. You can do it between jobs.

What happens to my calls if I cancel?

Depends which one you do.

Cancel and your smart assistant keeps working through the end of your current billing cycle — you’ve already paid for it, so it keeps answering until that period ends. After that, it stops.

Delete your account and it’s immediate: you’re billed right away and service ends the moment you delete.

Either way, one thing doesn’t happen automatically: your calls will keep forwarding to Bolt’s number until you turn forwarding off with your carrier. It’s the same code you used to turn it on — dial it again to send calls straight back to your phone.

Does Bolt replace me, or does it just help me keep up?

Backup, not a replacement. Bolt answers when you can’t, captures the details, sorts what’s urgent, and drafts your follow-ups. You stay in charge of every job — Bolt just makes sure nothing falls through the cracks while your hands are full.

Will it actually get the details right, or will I end up with garbage leads?

Bolt’s smart assistant captures what you’d want to know before calling someone back — name, callback number, address, what’s going on, and how urgent it is. And every single call comes with a full transcript, so if anything’s unclear or incomplete, you can see exactly what was said and fill in the gap yourself. Nothing’s hidden behind a summary you have to trust blindly.

How is this different from a human answering service or the AI receptionist built into my scheduling software?

A human answering service works, but it’s expensive and it’s off the clock sometimes too. The AI receptionist built into some scheduling platforms only works if you’re already locked into that platform’s ecosystem. Bolt doesn’t care what scheduling tool you use, or whether you use one at all — it works with your phone, your number, and your business, starting the day you sign up.
