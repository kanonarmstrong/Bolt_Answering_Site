The 5 Most Profitable Handyman Jobs You Can Start Offering Today

If you are still taking every random job that comes your way, you are probably leaving money on the table. The handymen who earn the most tend to pick a small set of repeatable jobs, get fast at them, and let their reputation do the marketing. Here are five of the best places to start, counted down from five to one, with tips on what makes each one pay.

5. Drywall repair

Drywall is high skill but needs very little material to get started. Plenty of homeowners cannot fix a hole themselves, and plenty of homes have holes. It is hard at first, but every patch makes you faster, and once you are known as the person who makes walls disappear, referrals follow.

4. Door and lock installation

Replacing doorknobs and deadbolts is simple work that many people would rather not touch. Doors are one of the things in a house that constantly break or get damaged, so demand never dries up. The tool list is short, which keeps your profit margin high, and you can become the go-to door person in your neighborhood.

3. Ceiling fans and light fixtures

Most residential fans and fixtures are close to identical, so each install teaches you something that applies to the next one. One warning: a fixture the customer calls “easy” can turn out to have a thousand tiny parts, and hanging them can take hours. Settle up front who handles these intricate fixtures or price that job differently.

2. Faucets and garbage disposals

The theme here is swapping, not building. Pulling out a kitchen faucet and putting in a new one is far quicker than anything that involves construction. Disposals are similar, since the problem is often a tripped breaker, a clog, or a simple replacement.

1. TV mounting and smart home installs

This category is big enough that some people build whole businesses on it: TVs, soundbars, video doorbells, smart thermostats, and a growing list of connected appliances. The work is cleaner than drywall or plumbing, customers usually buy the equipment, and it feels like a specialist service. Bigger TVs may need a second person, but otherwise the barrier is low.

The common thread

All five jobs are fast to learn, need few tools, are easy to repeat, and often let the customer supply the materials. Pick one or two, get very good at them, and you stop competing as a generalist and start stuffing your pockets.
