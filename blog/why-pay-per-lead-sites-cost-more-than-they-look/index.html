Why Pay-Per-Lead Sites Cost More Than They Look

The price on a pay-per-lead invoice is rarely the price of a job. You pay when a homeowner fills in a form, whether or not they are ready to hire and whether or not you are the only contractor who receives it.

The lead may be in several inboxes

In many lead-sale models, one homeowner request is sent to multiple contractors at once. So the moment you pay, you are racing others to a phone the homeowner may not answer.

The homeowner often filled in the form to see options, not to take a string of sales calls. That mismatch explains why so many paid leads never pick up, or already have a bid in hand and only want a price check.

Cost per lead is the wrong number

Plug in your own numbers. If a $40 lead turns into a $480 acquisition cost, the platform’s price tag was never the real price.

What contractors commonly report

Contractors who have bought leads describe a familiar pattern: prices that creep up, homeowners who never answer, leads that already have bids, and homeowners surprised to be called by several companies. Some describe trouble cancelling.

Regulators have also looked at the category. In January 2023 the FTC accepted a settlement with HomeAdvisor over allegations that it overstated how often its leads turned into jobs and misdescribed where some leads came from. The dispute ended in a $7.2 million settlement while the company did not admit wrongdoing (TechCrunch).

Read the terms before you buy

Before spending, ask three questions:

  1. Do you pay for leads that never answer?
  2. Can you cancel without a fee?
  3. Does the contract renew automatically?

Some contractors report being locked into multi-year agreements, or getting leads for the wrong kind of job. Get the answers in writing.

Keep the comparison fair, too. Google’s own Local Services Ads also charge per lead, so the same cost-per-job math applies there. Lead sites are not evil. They just should not be your only source, because you cannot control their price, their lead quality or how your reviews appear.

What to do instead

  1. Work out your cost per job won for every channel, using the table above.
  2. Put effort into channels you own: a clear website, a complete Google listing and steady reviews.
  3. Answer every call that comes in, from any source. A lead you never speak to cost money and returned nothing.
