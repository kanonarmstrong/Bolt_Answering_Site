How Specializing Lets a Handyman Charge More

When you are known for one thing, customers stop comparing you against the cheapest person on a TaskRabbit. They are buying certainty. You have done this exact job dozens of times, you show up with the right parts and tools, and you finish quickly. Speed is the part many new handymen miss: the faster you are, the higher the hourly rate you can earn even if the customer pays a fair flat price.

Let the customer bring the materials

A good way to keep your take home rate higher is by making sure the client supplies the necessary product or materials, such as a TV, a faucet, or a smart doorbell. That keeps your costs down and removes the headache of buying, returning, and marking up parts. The profit margin goes up because you are selling labor and skill, not inventory.

Set expectations before you arrive

Certain projects require a lot more time and can put your profit at risk. Decorative light fixtures with endless crystals can eat an afternoon, so agree beforehand who assembles them. And on a garbage disposal install with a dishwasher, knocking out the knockout plug is easy to forget (… trust us, we’ve all done it), so build it into your checklist. Small misses like these cost you a free return visit and a bit of goodwill.

Bundle and offer solutions customers did not know they needed

When someone books a TV mount, ask whether they want the wires hidden in the wall, and whether they would like a soundbar installed at the same time. Customers rarely think about cable runs until the TV is up and the wire is dangling. Solving a problem they did not know they had is where the extra revenue comes from, and it makes the job feel like a better experience.

A reality check before you raise your rates

Treat the hourly numbers as a goal, not a promise, and check your local rules first. Remember, some plumbing and electrical work may be restricted by licensing, so look up what your state or city allows before advertising swaps like faucets, disposals, or light fixtures.

A simple pricing approach

  1. Pick one or two niches from the list in the previous post.
  2. Track how long each job really takes over your first ten jobs.
  3. Quote a flat price based on your fastest reliable time, plus a margin for surprises.
  4. Add an optional add-on to every quote, such as hidden wires or a second fixture.
  5. Raise your price once you are booked out for a few weeks.
